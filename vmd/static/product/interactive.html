FAST PATH

Pose + motion

YOLO11 nano pose finds people and joints. Optical flow measures movement. Temporal rules look for persistent close interaction, possible falls, person down and hands up.

Default requested target: 20 FPS
BACKGROUND PATH

Objects + threats

YOLO26s supplies 80-class scene context. A separate YOLOv8n threat detector checks visible guns, knives, grenades and explosion-like visuals.

Routine sampling: up to 2 FPS
ADVISORY PATH

Relative depth

ZipDepth compares which tracked person appears nearer or farther in a sampled frame. It cannot measure metres or prove physical contact.

Routine sampling: 1 FPS

Routine specialist samples originate from capture; an event can also request its exact frame. Results retain their original timestamps. Current alerts are additive: pose rules, specialist threats and fresh temporal predictions can alert independently. General objects and depth are advisory; a learned fusion of all signals is future work.

04 / RESOURCE AWARENESS

Quiet moments.
Less model work.

Eco mode is optional and off by default. A lightweight motion gate reduces sampling only after ten continuous quiet seconds.

Calculation: quiet fraction × (1 − 1/10). Not measured device energy, accuracy or whole-pipeline savings.

Security checks keep running.

  1. Movement present: normal sampling and pose target.
  2. Ten quiet seconds: request pose and threats at 1 FPS; depth at 0.1 FPS.
  3. Movement returns: restore normal scheduling immediately.

Capture and motion monitoring still use resources. Busy scenes may save nothing. Lower scan rates can miss brief, small or obscured threats.

Long-term potential: lower average compute load and power use across quiet hours. Compare wall power, missed events and alert delay on identical footage before claiming electricity or carbon savings.

05 / ADAPT TO THE PLACE

Real scenes.
Reviewed learning.

A corridor, a station and a warehouse have different patterns. The optional clip classifier learns from the site's data while existing detectors remain active.

Raw clips + proposed labels→Operator review→ResNet18 encoder→64-unit GRU→Held-out evaluation

A candidate, then a decision.

ResNet18 remains frozen; the GRU head learns across 16 sampled frames. Reviewed examples carry more weight. Exact duplicates are collapsed and conflicting duplicates excluded. Unreviewed model-generated labels cannot train themselves.

Training uses AdamW, a 0.001 learning rate, batch size 8 and 30 epochs. Feature caching avoids repeatedly encoding unchanged clips. This trains an event classifier, not the YOLO detectors or depth model.

Improvement must be demonstrated.

Three normal clips and two clips of one event type can start a candidate, but do not establish quality. Promotion needs reviewed data across at least three source groups, an independent held-out source, at least 80% event recall and 60% precision, plus class coverage and non-regression checks.

A passing candidate can activate. Otherwise the current model stays. Unseen event types, poor labels and similar clips from the same recording can make validation misleading. Keep a separate final test set.

06 / WHAT CREATES VALUE

A complete operating loop.

The distinction is how these pieces work together. These are product strengths, not a claim that no other system offers them.

01

Evidence at the decision point

Operators inspect the relevant moment and reasons, then record their decision. Measure review time and evidence playback success.

02

Local operation

Inference and evidence can stay on site after dependencies and models are installed. Measure network reliance and recovery after outages.

03

Traceable results

A delayed result stays attached to the original frame. Measure capture-to-result delay, stale results and missed incidents.

04

Controlled adaptation

Learning adds local context without allowing a normal prediction to suppress rule or weapon alerts. Compare class recall and false alerts per camera-hour.

How the cables and accounts fit

IP cameras → Ethernet / RTSPUSB camera → USBVMD edge computerHDMI / DisplayPort → monitor

Planned shared access: the website owner creates users and camera groups; the device receives access rules securely. A user signs in on the authorised local network and sees only permitted cameras and evidence. Wired Ethernet can work too. Shared Wi-Fi alone gives no permission, and guest-network isolation may prevent access.

Today the app binds to localhost with optional single-account password protection. Multi-user roles, licence services, secure LAN viewing and access-rule synchronisation are planned. Offline sign-in, licence grace periods and permission revocation need explicit product design.

08 / PLAN A PILOT

Size the workload.
Then price the device.

A compact edge computer can do the work; a basic microcontroller cannot run this full stack. The calculator uses editable budget assumptions, not current supplier quotes.

Your planning assumptions

Starting specifications to test

Small pilot: modern six-core CPU class, 16 GB memory, 256–512 GB SSD, reliable Ethernet and cooling. The repository's older allowance is ₹70,000–₹150,000 per computer, not a market quote or throughput guarantee.

Higher load: consider 32 GB RAM and a CUDA GPU on Linux. The older larger allowance is ₹100,000–₹220,000. ARM appliances need dependency and full-pipeline validation. macOS currently uses CPU inference.

Before a buyer commits

Confirm stream compatibility, concurrent cameras, resolution, target analysis rate, required incidents, retention, operator access and response policy. Include network switches, storage expansion, backup power, display, installation and support.

No validated hardware profiles ship with the project. Test simultaneous cameras, night scenes, crowding, evidence writes, reconnects and heat. Extra devices need separate app instances; there is no fleet coordinator today. Licence price has not been set.
