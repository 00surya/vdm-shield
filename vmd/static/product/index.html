01
PRESENTATION TOPIC

What is VDM?

Existing cameras become an assisted safety workflow.

Imagine an operator watching several CCTV screens. Most footage is ordinary. A short incident at an entrance is easy to miss. VDM processes video locally, highlights possible incidents and saves evidence so the operator can make an informed decision.

  1. ObserveA camera or uploaded video provides the scene.
  2. UnderstandPeople, motion and specialist checks supply signals.
  3. ReviewAn operator sees evidence and decides what happened.
  4. ImproveReviewed examples can train a local event model.

The problem

More footage than people can watch carefully; slow searches and alerts without context.

Our solution

A local analysis device, a browser workspace, short evidence clips and a review workflow.

Who benefits

Security teams at campuses, warehouses, hospitals and similar sites. Each setting needs its own acceptance tests.

For the design team Open with one relatable incident. Show the operator receiving the useful moment, not a claim of automatic certainty.

Complete end-to-end workflow

Follow a frame, then follow the evidence.

  1. 01 · ConnectUpload a recording, select a USB webcam, or enter an HTTP/RTSP stream.
  2. 02 · IdentifyCapture assigns camera, session, frame number and source time.
  3. 03 · ProcessPose/motion runs alongside sampled object and depth workers.
  4. 04 · ProposeRules, specialist threats and an active clip model can create alerts.
  1. 05 · PreserveSave available pre-event and following footage, reasons and metadata.
  2. 06 · ReviewConfirm, dismiss, undo review or correct the training label.
  3. 07 · LearnBuild a candidate from clips; evaluate before activation.
  4. 08 · MonitorWatch camera health, delay, evidence writing and storage.

Timing matters: a worker may finish later, but its result keeps the original frame identity. Routine object samples come from capture; depth samples use frames with matching pose detections. A pose event may request its exact frame as a priority sample.

PRESENTATION TOPIC

What every AI component contributes

Several observers answer different questions.

YOLO11 nano pose

Finds people and body joints; supplies tracking geometry. Pose and motion target 20 FPS by default, but hardware determines completed FPS.

Optical flow + rules

Measures movement and checks persistent patterns: possible fight, possible fall, person down, hands up and camera-local motion anomalies.

ZipDepth

Routine depth at 1 FPS with a per-person relative Z axis. Compares nearer/farther order for close people in a sampled frame. Relative depth is not metres or proof of contact.

YOLO26s general objects

Recognises 80 COCO classes for context: people, vehicles, bags and everyday objects. Its knife class does not independently raise a weapon alarm.

Specialist threat detector

YOLOv8n detector for visible guns, knives, grenades and explosion-like visuals. Shares the routine object worker, sampled up to 2 FPS.

ResNet18 + GRU

An optional temporal event classifier trained on clips. Adds predictions for its learned labels; existing rule and weapon alerts remain active.

Current combination is additive, not a single calibrated model that fuses everything. A normal clip prediction cannot cancel a rule or weapon alert. Robbery, chain snatching and accident labels in the interface do not imply validated automatic detection of those events.

For the design team Give each model a question: who is here, how are they moving, who is nearer, what is visible, and what does this clip resemble?

05
PRESENTATION TOPIC

All operator features

One workspace for live observation, evidence, review and improvement.

Upload & camera setup

Upload supported video files or add USB / HTTP / RTSP sources. Name cameras, start/stop analysis and inspect progress or frame freshness.

Three analysis views

Pose/motion, sampled depth and sampled objects/threats. Different rates and result ages are visible; a smooth screen does not prove every frame was analysed.

Alerts & evidence

Suggested label, source, time, score, priority, reasons and available raw/annotated evidence. Repeated nearby signals can become one grouped incident.

Human review

Confirm an incident, dismiss a false positive, undo review or correct a label. Preserve the difference between detector proposal and operator judgement.

Recordings & history

Manage saved videos, inspect evidence frames and download clips. Search events by date/time, text, priority and review state. History starts 12 hours after record creation.

Retention

Default seven-day expiry for new automatic evidence. Changing retention does not rewrite existing expiry dates. Records and uploads remain; continuous recording is not implemented.

Training & data quality

Review class balance, examples, duplicates and evaluation. Train a candidate in the background and keep the active model if promotion fails.

Help & recovery

Local help includes regional-language drafts; main controls remain English. Camera definitions persist and enabled failed streams retry. Removal of a source does not erase evidence.

For the design team Group features around operator actions: connect, watch, verify, find, improve and maintain. Avoid a screen full of model names.

Analytics and device monitoring

Show what the site reports and whether the system is keeping up.

Event analytics

Event types, camera activity, people counts, review outcomes and label distribution help operators inspect patterns and pending work.

Pipeline measurements

Input FPS, completed pose/motion rate, depth and object-worker rates, processing time, result age and capture-to-result delay.

Device & evidence health

Camera freshness, worker readiness, filesystem free space, service uptime, write queue, dropped records and evidence-saving errors.

Operational logs

Camera state changes and service problems support troubleshooting. These logs are not a full user security audit trail.

Learning evaluation

Dataset coverage, candidate state, validation precision and recall, per-class support and active model information.

Future monitoring

Full CPU/temperature telemetry, fleet dashboards, enterprise audit trails and managed updates need further implementation.

  1. MeasureCollect events and operational telemetry.
  2. ExplainShow patterns, delays and status in readable terms.
  3. RespondReview evidence or investigate a failed source.
  4. VerifyCheck that the issue is resolved.

Event charts are not detection accuracy. Measure missed incidents against independent labelled footage. “On pace” describes processing throughput, not whether every incident is detected.

07
PRESENTATION TOPIC

Extraordinary value: optional Eco mode

Less model work during quiet periods, with periodic checks continuing.

  1. Active sceneNormal pose target and routine specialist sampling.
  2. 10 quiet secondsLightweight motion monitoring finds no qualifying change.
  3. Quiet schedulePose 1 FPS · threats 1 FPS · depth 0.1 FPS.
  4. Motion returnsNormal processing schedule resumes.

Why it matters

A quiet corridor can request fewer expensive model scans. Across many quiet hours, lower average load may reduce power and heat.

Illustrative comparison

Assume normal pose runs at 10 FPS and the camera spends 60% of time quiet. Eco average = 0.4 × 10 + 0.6 × 1 = 4.6 FPS: 54% fewer pose scans.

What the app measures

Estimated avoided pose work, actual Eco scans and quiet time. Capture, decoding and motion monitoring still consume resources. Busy scenes may save nothing.

Tradeoff and proof

Sampled checks can miss brief or obscured threats. Compare wall power, recall, missed events and delay using the same scenes before making environmental claims.

Eco is off by default, enabled per live camera. Uploads receive normal processing. Model-work reduction does not equal a measured reduction in electricity or carbon emissions.

For the design team Show both schedules side by side with dots representing scans. Mark the 54% example as an assumption, not a measured result.

PRESENTATION TOPIC

Extraordinary value: site- and scene-based learning

Reviewed examples can improve recognition at the physical site where the system is used.

  1. CollectIncident clips and provisional normal examples.
  2. ReviewConfirm or correct the proposed labels.
  3. Encode & trainFrozen ResNet18 → 64-unit GRU head.
  4. EvaluateIndependent source holdout and class checks.
  5. DecidePass: activate. Otherwise retain current model.

Learn the physical site

Reviewed clips expose the temporal classifier to the site’s camera angles, lighting, background, crowd density and normal activity. A warehouse entrance and a hospital corridor can have different visual patterns.

How quality can improve

Representative local examples may reduce false alarms and improve recognition of supported events in that setting. This is adaptation through clip training, not automatic understanding of a place or guaranteed improvement.

Prove it on unseen recordings

Keep complete recording sessions separate between training and testing, including different days, lighting and camera viewpoints. Measure event recall, false alerts and delay. Background memorisation can look accurate on familiar clips but fail on a new scene.

What is available today

The current learner trains a shared active temporal classifier using stored clips. Separate per-camera or per-site model routing is not implemented. Moving a camera or changing the scene calls for fresh evaluation; general-purpose pose, object and depth models remain unchanged.

Training setup

16 sampled frames per clip; 512-dimensional features; AdamW, learning rate 0.001, batch size 8 and 30 epochs. Feature caching reuses unchanged clip encodings.

Data protections

Reviewed clips carry more weight. Exact duplicates are collapsed and conflicting duplicates excluded. Unreviewed model-generated labels do not teach themselves.

Promotion requirements

At least 20 reviewed clips across three source groups and a suitable holdout; aggregate event recall ≥80%, precision ≥60%; at least five held-out clips per class and ≥80% class recall, plus non-regression checks.

Practical limits

Three normal and two event clips only meet the candidate-training minimum. They do not prove quality. New classes, scene changes and biased labels need representative evaluation. Training does not fine-tune YOLO or ZipDepth.

For the design team Draw a decision diamond after evaluation with PASS and WAIT branches. The old active model remains until a candidate passes.

Website, download, licence and group access

The proposed commercial onboarding journey.

◆ PLANNED — The current prototype does not implement the website account, licensing, multiple users, groups or secure shared-network access described here.

  1. Organisation signupCreate the buyer account on the website.
  2. DownloadReceive signed software for supported hardware.
  3. ActivateEnter licence key; verify device entitlement.
  4. Device OwnerVerify the owner and create the first administrator.
  1. Create groupsOwner invites users and assigns roles on the website.
  2. Assign camerasChoose which group may view each camera and evidence.
  3. Sync accessDevice securely receives the current permissions.
  4. Sign in locallyPermitted users on the reachable LAN open their cameras.

Licence and login are different

The activation key grants software entitlement. It must not be the administrator password. Licence limits, fees and renewal policy are product decisions still to define.

Group example

Gate operators → entrance cameras. Parking team → parking cameras. Supervisor → assigned cameras plus review. Viewer → permitted viewing only. These roles are proposed.

Network rule

Same Wi-Fi is insufficient permission. Users need authenticated, camera-scoped access; wired Ethernet on the permitted network can also work. Guest Wi-Fi isolation may block connectivity.

Offline operation

Define offline activation, signed local entitlement, licence grace period, local login and how revoked access reaches an offline device. A website outage should have an explicit operational policy.

For the design team Use two parallel lanes: website manages organisation/licence/access; edge device processes and stores video. Connect them with activation and permission-sync arrows, not a cloud video upload arrow.

How to run VDM today

The working local prototype setup, separate from the planned licence journey.

  1. PrepareFollow README.md: Python 3.10–3.13, dependencies and verified model files.
  2. LaunchRun ./start.command from the project folder.
  3. OpenVisit http://127.0.0.1:8765 on the processing computer.
  4. ConnectUpload a video or enter a camera stream / webcam index.
  5. VerifyCheck worker readiness, frame progress, evidence saving and playback.

Access today

Localhost binding; optional provisioned operator password through scripts/provision_access.py. There are no separate owner/group accounts in the current app.

Attached display

HDMI or DisplayPort connects the processing device to a monitor. device.py provides a prototype browser-kiosk launcher; OS provisioning is still required.

Camera connection

IP video arrives by Ethernet/network using a stream endpoint. USB cameras connect directly. HDMI does not connect an IP camera feed to the analysis software.

First acceptance check

Process known footage, review the result, verify evidence playback, disconnect/reconnect a camera and observe processing rates before adding more feeds.

Hardware, estimated cost and buying decisions

Budget assumptions first; measured workload determines the device.

Small pilot starting point

Modern multi-core CPU, approximately 16 GB RAM, 256–512 GB SSD, Ethernet, cooling and reliable power. A GPU is a benchmark decision, not automatically required for every pilot.

Higher load starting point

Consider 32 GB RAM, more CPU capacity and a CUDA GPU on Linux. macOS currently uses CPU. ARM/Jetson deployment needs full compatibility and throughput testing.

Historical planning allowances

The repository uses ₹70,000–₹150,000 for its smaller computer band and ₹100,000–₹220,000 for the larger band. These are dated internal allowances, not current quotes or validated camera capacities.

Example equipment budget

Assume one computer at ₹70,000 with base SSD, two existing cameras at ₹0 incremental cost and ₹10,000 other equipment: ₹80,000 total. Licence, installation, maintenance and electricity are excluded.

  1. Buyer inputsCamera count, resolution, FPS, event classes and access.
  2. Storage policyEvent frequency, clip length and retention days.
  3. BenchmarkAll workers, concurrent cameras, writes, heat and night scenes.
  4. QuoteComputer + cameras + network + storage + display + support.

Storage example: 4 cameras × 7 days × [(5 events × 15 seconds × 12 Mbps ÷ 8,000) + (24 normal clips × 10 seconds × 4 Mbps ÷ 8,000)] ≈ 6.5 GB of planned clips. These are assumed aggregate bitrates/sample volumes. Add OS/model space and free reserve. No continuous recording is implemented.

For the design team Keep price assumptions and exclusions beside the numbers. The four-source process limit does not certify four-camera performance; no validated device profiles ship with the project.

PRESENTATION TOPIC

Impact, differentiation and proof

Explain the benefit, then name how it will be measured.

Faster understanding

An operator receives the relevant clip and reasons. Measure review time and evidence availability rather than promising a percentage improvement.

Accountable decisions

Human corrections remain distinguishable from model proposals. Future user-level audits and roles would deepen accountability.

Local resilience

On-site inference reduces cloud dependence. Validate reconnects, power recovery, storage pressure and recovery from failed workers.

Measured efficiency

Eco can reduce quiet-scene inference work. Verify actual power use and safety tradeoffs on the target hardware.

Better local fit

Reviewed temporal training may adapt event recognition to a site. Compare held-out recall, false alerts per camera-hour and delay against the current system.

What makes the combination valuable

Frame provenance + evidence review + visible operational health + conservative Eco scheduling + gated site learning. These are integrated product strengths; market uniqueness has not been proven.

The promise: help an operator notice important moments, inspect the evidence and improve from reviewed reality. Small, hidden or blurred threats, unfamiliar events and incomplete training data remain real limitations.

For the design team Close with the operator outcome. Use pilot evidence for accuracy, cost and sustainability claims, not model marketing figures.
